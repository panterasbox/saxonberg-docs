## The account

EotL was run by **Duncan**, on an unusual mandate. The founder's words:

> *"He had two rules that he enforced emphatically: 1. don't piss off
> anyone bigger than you, 2. have fun. Beyond that there was a power
> structure of discrete levels with different read/write permissions, and
> if anyone ever tried to make a 'rulebook' beyond Duncan's maxims he
> actively swatted it down. Otherwise, he remained completely absent from
> the power structures."*

And on how it felt from inside:

> *"We thought we were a meritocracy but were kidding ourselves, like
> most."*

> *"Honestly my favorite game I've ever played as an executive."*

That is the whole primary record. Four moves:

1. **Two maxims, enforced emphatically.** One is a purpose — *have fun*.
   One is a conflict rule that delegates settlement to power — *don't piss
   off anyone bigger than you*.
2. **Everything else was code.** Discrete levels with read/write
   permissions. The entire enforcement apparatus was permission bits on
   the mudlib. There was no law above the bits except the two maxims.
3. **No rulebook — as policy, not neglect.** Anyone who tried to write
   law above the maxims was actively stopped.
4. **Absent from the power structures otherwise.** Present as the
   enforcer of two rules; gone from everything else.

## Reading — what it got right

*(A reading, not the record.)*

Duncan's mandate is a **self-minimizing executive** thirty years before
the phrase — the shape the executive slate
names as *law = code; the executive's prime directive is reducing its
own authority to only what genuinely needs a human.* He kept exactly one
human residue — emphatic enforcement of two maxims — and left everything
else to the bits.

The **anti-rulebook** instinct is *correct* under law = code. The
rulebook he swatted down is precisely the class of rule that needs a
human to enforce it — rules that create jurisdiction, adjudication, and
a politics of who gets to read them at whom. He did not swat down
permissions. He swatted down rules-that-need-judges. That is the same
distinction the Compact draws between what code enforces and what a
person administers (Art. V §6, §9).

And the two maxims were **few**. A polity with two laws it actually
enforces is more governed than one with two hundred it doesn't.

## Reading — where it failed, and why

The failure was not the minimalism. It was two specific things:

1. ⭐ **The conflict maxim sanctified size.** *Don't provoke the strong*
   means the strong settle disputes, so getting strong *is* the merit, so
   "rising by integrating" means accruing power and calling it earned.
   The meritocracy was a mask not because anyone lied but because the
   rule made strength self-legitimating. The bible's line — *the poison
   was already in the wine* — is this.
2. ⭐ **The head was absent from the one place a head cannot be.** Not the
   power structures — he was right to stay out of those — but the **check
   on accrual**. There was none, and he would not be it. With no written
   law and no check, rule 1 plus absence *computes to* the Pooling. It
   needs no villain; the bible names one (Corvin Solus) and then says the
   lesson stays structural.

## What the Compact keeps, and what it swaps

| Duncan | the Compact |
|---|---|
| two maxims, enforced emphatically | **the eternity clauses** — few, entrenched; the executive's one non-delegable job is that they hold |
| everything else is permission bits | **law = code**; the executive shrinks its own discretion into tools, and the executive log's empty-authorization rows are the meter |
| no rulebook | **no rulebook** — a rule that needs a human to enforce it is a bug report against the executive |
| *have fun* | **theater is the product** — the committees put on a good show; the executive's job is that the show goes on |
| *don't provoke the strong* | ⭐ **swapped.** Standing is a *rate*, tenure has *terms*, title is held by activity, the record is total. Size is never self-legitimating because nothing accrues without a clock on it — *the platform never decides who, only how long* |
| absent from the power structures | absent from the power structures — **but present as the check.** The log, the reports, the Schedule tiers are the head being present *without participating*: the Warden who intervenes, mechanically, every time |

That last row is the design. Duncan was right that the head should stay
out of the politics. He was wrong that staying out meant nothing checks
accrual. The Compact's answer is that the check is not a person — it is
clocks and a record — so the head can be absent from power *and* the
accrual still gets checked.

## What the Compact does not do

It does not adopt rule 1 in any costume. Every place a design here has
been tempted toward "the strong settle it" — enforceable joint liability
between partners, a rulebook of who may read whom, a rating that is the
only rater — it has been refused for the same reason the First Law is
the seed of the Pooling. The non-aggression EotL got from prudence, the
Compact gets from non-recourse, the 13th module, and the property floor.
