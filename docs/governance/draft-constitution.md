# Draft Constitution of the Compact

> **Status: DRAFT — not ratified.** A formal consolidation of the design in
> cooperative-slate.md into normative articles. The
> slate carries the *rationale*; this document carries the *provisions*. Five
> genuinely-undecided points are marked **[OPEN]** with a recommended
> resolution; everything else reflects the settled design. Ratification and
> the transition from founder-fiat are governed by Article XI. The articles are
> the *logic*; every tunable value they reference lives once in the **Schedule
> of Parameters** at the end, each with a change-tier, its value **set at
> ratification** (game-balance rows *calibrated at launch*).
>
> **What belongs here — the three-floor test.** A clause is constitutional
> only if it survives one question: *would a legitimate operator who declares
> "you have no rights" still need it?* Three floors survive; everything else is
> legislation or left to ratification.
>
> 1. **The firewall** — stake-is-not-stock, no cash-out, no pay-to-win, no
>    dividend. Kept not because the polity "wouldn't function" without it, but
>    because dropping it means running an unregistered security or a gambling
>    house: the test here is *can you operate this without a lawyer.* Even the
>    most rights-stripping operator keeps the firewall, out of self-interest.
>    (Eternity.)
> 2. **The machine and its tools** — that chambers, influence, a legislature,
>    an executive, a judiciary, a tamper-evident record, and a defined
>    amendment process *exist*; and that the executive **provides the tools of
>    self-government** — an ungameable deliberation surface (Art. V §8), the
>    machinery of egalitarian justice (Art. VI §4) — built and entrenched, even
>    though a polity may decline to use them and run on operator fiat instead.
>    The tools must *exist and be available*; whether to convene them is the
>    polity's choice. Strip every civil right and the machine still stands.
> 3. **Everything else is deferred.** Civil rights (to ratification); the
>    economic system, the institution roster, and the choice of specific
>    products (to legislation). A clause that names a *specific means* — which
>    institutions, which currency, which polling tool — states a default the
>    polity may replace, never an invariant.
>
> Below the ratification threshold (Art. XI) an instance runs on operator fiat:
> a community may be "you have no rights" indefinitely — a streamer doing their
> own code review and their own ban appeals — with the machine's constraints
> and the civil rights binding only once it grows into a republic and ratifies.
> And ratifying a right is often nothing more than removing operator discretion
> over a tool the executive already provides: **due process** widens the
> judicial jury pool from the operator to a sortition of equals and binds the
> operator to the verdict (Art. VI) — the process was always there, a streamer's
> ban appeals are it at a pool of one; **free expression** binds the
> deliberation surface (Art. V §8). The executive builds the tools once; the
> convention decides where the operator's discretion ends.
>
> **The kernel and the library.** This constitution is deliberately the
> *bare-bones kernel* that ships to every community on the platform — the
> firewall, the machine, and the provided tools, nothing more. The choices it
> defers (which rights to compel, which economy to run, which institutions to
> charter) are *not* meant to be re-solved from scratch by every community.
> They are drawn from a shared **library of model amendments** — pre-drafted,
> composable "political legos" a community adopts through the ordinary Art. X
> process when it onboards or grows into a republic. The kernel is common to
> all; the library is how communities differ *without each reinventing due
> process.* The catalog and its design live in
> amendment-library-slate.md.

## Preamble

We, the members of this world — its makers, its funders, and its players —
establish this constitution to govern ourselves as **the Compact**: a
polity in which those who fund the world are those who govern it, and in
which the act of governing is itself part of the world. We take our name
from what we hold to be the whole of the thing: not the apparatus but the
agreement, engaged in good faith — for a right that lives only on paper
is no right at all. We hold that a
digital polity, freed of the distances and frictions of physical assembly,
can govern *better* than its forebears — keeping what those systems protected
and discarding only what they merely endured. We bind ourselves to honest
measure over hidden authority, to power that is earned and spent rather than
owned, to a record that cannot be falsified, and to a structure that outlasts
any of us — including its author.

---

## Article I — Foundational Principles

These are the foundations of the polity. They are eternity clauses,
unamendable except by founding anew (Article X §4).

1. **Stake is not stock.** Membership confers governance and recognition; it
   never confers financial ownership, profit, or any redeemable claim on
   money. No stake is bought, sold, or cashed out.
2. **No money buys advantage.** Real money may fund the world and earn a
   *voice* in its governance; it may never purchase in-world currency,
   property, or any gameplay advantage.
3. **Power is earned and spent, never owned.** Influence is acquired through
   contribution, exercised through participation, and replenished only by
   continued contribution. It is never a fixed, transferable holding.
4. **No number is an authority.** No tally — of coin, of quality, of votes,
   of regard — shall stand as an authoritative verdict on worth, truth, or
   merit. Tallies may be counted; they never rule.
5. **Co-equality.** No chamber of the legislature is supreme over another,
   and no class of contribution may be disenfranchised.
6. **The integrity of the record is independent of its keeper.** The archive
   of governance shall be **tamper-evident and universally verifiable**, so
   that no operator — not even the branch that runs it — can falsify it
   undetectably. Every member may verify; no act may rest on a falsified
   record.
7. **Enforcement is by code, not discretion.** Where the law is mechanically
   applicable it shall be enforced by code — uniformly, automatically, and
   without selective discretion. Human enforcement is reserved for matters of
   judgment and is always subject to review. No power of enforcement is
   arbitrary. Enforcement is mechanical; **administration — the judgment of how
   to run the polity — is not, and is never automated** (Art. V §9).

## Article II — Membership & Citizenship  **[OPEN]**

> **Recommendation.** Membership is open to any real, Sybil-resistant human,
> *earned by contribution* of any of the three kinds (creation, funding,
> participation) and gated only by a participation threshold — never by
> wealth or invitation. A member who meets the threshold is a **citizen**,
> bearing influence and the franchise; the broad participant base is the
> foundational citizenry. No citizen may be stripped of membership except by
> due process (Article IX).

## Article III — Influence

1. Governance is exercised through **influence**, of three non-fungible
   kinds, each earned by a kind of contribution: **producer** (creation —
   measured by the engagement one's authored content earns), **capital**
   (funding), **consumer** (participation — engaged consumption, measured as
   engagement × regard).
2. Each kind is exercised only within its own chamber. No kind may be
   converted into another, nor into office in any branch.
3. Influence is spent or allocated in the exercise of governance and
   replenished only by continued contribution (Art. I §3). Absence is never
   penalized; only the faucet stops.
4. The method by which influence becomes a decision is **fixed by this
   constitution** (Art. IV §2), not chosen chamber-by-chamber: a chamber is the
   whole of its membership, with no prior method to vote a new one in, and the
   system is engineered for the game's stability — so it is kernel-given. A
   chamber governs only its **internal organization** (Art. IV §6), never the
   method itself.
5. **Influence weights the vote, never the argument.** In deliberation every
   member contributes as an equal, in one voice; influence enters only at the
   ballot, never in the reasoning that precedes it.
6. **Renown weights earned influence; it is never authority on its own.**
   Regard — the community's conduct-earned recognition — may **multiply** the
   influence a member earns by participating (notably consumer influence, via
   `engagement × regard`), within bounds; it never mints standalone voting
   weight, grants office, or organizes deliberation. *Reputation → bounded
   weight; never reputation → authority.*
7. **Producer influence may be supplemented by merit pay.** Because producers
   largely contribute unpaid, a bounded **merit award of producer influence —
   and producer influence only, never consumer or capital** — may recognize
   creation **the §1 measure can't capture**: work the instrumentation can't
   *see* (infrastructure), or worth it can't *judge* (whether content betters
   the world is a human call, not a code metric). The **exception**, never the
   main way producer influence is earned. It is **minted and capped by the
   legislature**, **evaluated by competence** (good work judged by those able
   to judge it), **recusal-gated** (no self-award), **sub-decisive**, and
   recorded with a public rationale and **justiciable**.

## Article IV — The Legislature

1. The legislature comprises **three co-equal chambers**: the Producer House,
   the Capital House, and the Consumer House.
2. **How influence is held and spent.** Influence is not a one-time grant but
   a **stock**: each kind sits in a **reservoir** whose ceiling is set by the
   member's *lifetime* contribution and which **regenerates** with continued
   contribution up to that ceiling — absence stops the regeneration, it never
   confiscates the reservoir. A member governs by **allocating** influence onto
   the bills before the floor, and allocation is **time-weighted**
   (conviction-style): the weight an allocation carries **builds the longer it
   rests on a position and decays when moved**, so influence is spent as
   *sustained commitment*, not a momentary dump. Each house tallies its **own
   kind** of allocated weight, by **count, not price** (Art. I §4) and by no
   gameable ranking; a member allocates each kind independently and may back a
   bill differently across houses. This system is **kernel-fixed and uniform**,
   not chosen chamber-by-chamber — a chamber being its whole membership, with no
   prior method to choose one, and the rule engineered for stability. Its
   **constants** (reservoir ceiling and shape, regeneration and decay rates, the
   thresholds below) are organic law (§7), and the precise reconciliation of a
   capped, regenerating reservoir with continuous allocation **remains to be
   specified**; the engineered design is in
   cooperative-slate.md §§ *Influence* and *The
   legislature*.
3. **Bills are global and concurrent.** There is one central floor of bills;
   every bill is deliberated globally and weighed by all houses at once, with
   **no originating house** — membership being shared, which house raised a bill
   carries no weight. A bill **carries in a house** when its allocated weight
   crosses that house's **passage threshold** and holds there through the
   conviction build; it **becomes law** on a **majority of houses** (both of
   two; two of three). Higher tiers raise the bar (Art. X). A house below a
   **quorum** of active members cannot legitimately decide: it **abstains** —
   counting toward neither passage nor blocking. Passage is always measured
   against the **full** count of houses, never a shrunken one — so an abstaining
   house must be made up by those present (one sparse house → the other two must
   both assent), and the polity can **never collapse to single-house rule.**
   Should too few houses remain quorate to reach a majority, ordinary law cannot
   pass until the constituencies recover; sustained sub-viability falls back to
   **caretaker governance** — the operator/root-power floor, as below
   ratification (Art. XI) — the population ladder run in reverse.
4. **A bill's life on the floor is finite.** A member **tables** a bill by
   committing a minimal **sponsoring allocation**; without a sponsor it never
   reaches the floor. A tabled bill **lives** while its support holds above a
   **survival floor** and is deliberated; it **lapses** — dying on the floor —
   if support falls below that floor (abandonment) or it reaches a **maximum
   lifespan** without carrying. To persist past the maximum, its supporters must
   pass a **continuing resolution** — a deliberate renewal carrying its own
   sustained support; otherwise the bill must be re-tabled afresh. The survival
   floor, the maximum lifespan, and the renewal bar are organic law (§7).
5. The legislature enacts law as **requirements** — what the rule is and the
   intent it serves — not as implementation; it may fix specific
   **invariants** to be guaranteed (and may supply code for those). The
   implementation of law — the voting system included — is an executive
   function (Art. V).
6. A chamber governs only its **internal organization** — representation
   (direct or representational), officers, rules committee, deliberation norms;
   such bylaws are not constitutional law and never reach the **voting method**,
   which §2 fixes. Constituencies may **caucus** to form an opt-in position, but
   no caucus binds a member's vote.
7. The **structure** of the voting system (§§2–4) changes only by
   **constitutional amendment** (Art. X); its **tuning constants** — reservoir
   caps, regeneration and decay rates, passage thresholds, bill lifespan — are
   **organic law** (collected, with every other tunable value, in the
   *Schedule of Parameters*), so the game stays balanceable without the
   structure being touched.

## Article V — The Executive

1. Executive power is held by a **Prime Minister**, who holds office by
   commanding the **confidence of a majority of chambers**.
2. The Prime Minister is installed by investiture (a bill) and removed only
   by **constructive no-confidence** (a majority of chambers naming a
   successor). The office **may** be term-limited by law, but need not be —
   confidence is the standing accountability.
3. The executive **executes only what the legislature has authorized**; it
   sets no policy of its own and commands no power the law has not granted.
4. The administration is a **civil service of chartered institutions**, each
   with a bounded mandate, its own staffing, judicial oversight, and
   durability beyond any officeholder — institutions, not hierarchies.
   *Which* institutions exist is established by legislation.
5. Any **enforcement institution** is chartered by the legislature and
   reviewable by the judiciary; it is never the personal instrument of the
   Prime Minister.
6. The executive **implements** the legislature's requirements and **enforces
   the law by code** wherever the law is mechanically applicable — uniformly
   and without discretion. Programming the machine is an executive function,
   requiring specialized knowledge; that the implementation conforms to the
   requirements is verified by the judiciary (Art. VI).
7. **Human enforcement** is reserved for matters of judgment that code cannot
   decide; it is bounded, recorded, recusal-gated, and subject to review. No
   enforcement is arbitrary or selective.
8. The executive shall **provide and maintain a structured deliberation
   surface** for bills before the legislature — one whose organization is the
   argument's own structure, not a ranking of persons or contributions by user
   signal — open to every member. It need not be the only venue for
   discussion, and no member is compelled to use it; but the ungameable
   surface shall always exist and be available to all.
9. **The executive *administers* — and administration is human.** Code
   *enforces* the law (§6); it does not *administer* it. The judgment of how to
   run the instance is irreducibly human — the polity will never code its way
   out of it. The operating staff exercise that judgment under **bounded
   mandate**, **confidence** (§1), and **judicial review**; the polity shapes it
   only by **tuning the levers** — the values in the *Schedule of Parameters* —
   and by oversight, never by replacing the judgment with code. Automation
   reaches the **mechanical**; the administrative hand stays a person's.

## Article VI — The Judiciary

1. The judiciary runs **one process at every scale**, which the executive
   shall **provide and maintain** (cf. Art. V §8): an **asynchronous case** —
   a matter is filed with its argument, a **verdict** is rendered by a
   deadline, a no-show yields judgment on the record, a live hearing optional.
   The shape is the same whether the matter is a code review, a ban appeal, or
   a full trial — so a community that already hears appeals adopts *no new
   procedure*, only this process's wider settings.
2. The process judges **verification** (conformance to the letter — that an
   implementation meets the requirement it claims; *code review*) and
   **spirit** (conformance to the purpose — that an act was fair; *the
   appeal*); a consequential act may be tested on both.
3. Only two things **scale with the polity**:
   - **the jury pool** — from a **pool of one** (the operator, hearing their
     own cases) to a **sortition of the citizenry** drawn by lot; a streamer's
     existing appeal flow *is* this process with the pool set to themselves;
     and
   - **bindingness** — from **operator-overridable** (the verdict advises an
     operator who may overrule it) to **operator-bound** (the verdict is
     final).
4. **When the jury pool is the citizenry it is egalitarian** — drawn by lot,
   influence and reputation counting for nothing; the spirit/appeals face from
   the flat citizenry (past a tenure threshold), the verification face from a
   competence-measured pool. Its verdicts are cast through the case's own
   argument structure under fixed rules of sound reasoning — completeness,
   consistency, grounding, conformance to law, freedom from forbidden basis —
   none dictating the *conclusion*.
5. A ratified **due-process** right (Art. IX) sets both knobs to their republic
   positions: it widens the jury pool to sortition and makes the verdict bind
   the operator. It **introduces no process** — the process was always there —
   it changes *who judges* and *removes the override*.
6. The judiciary exercises **constitutional review**, voiding any act or law
   that infringes an entrenched protection or this constitution.
7. **Abatement — taking live content offline.** Removing live, greenlit content
   is a **deprivation**, and demands *more* process than a greenlight's denial,
   not less: live content holds a **presumption** earned by passing review and
   by use, so the burden falls on the challenger. A **fault**-based takedown
   (broken, harmful, exploitative) runs as a **case** — the same two faces
   judging whether the content still verifies and still serves the spirit — and
   yields a **cure order**: the producer is told the specific defect and given a
   **remediation window**, the content going dark only if uncured. Pulling for
   fault **claws back** the influence that content earned (it was never
   legitimately earned); retiring conforming content is **no-fault deprecation**
   — ordinary legislation (a sunset), under which earned influence merely
   **decays naturally**, never clawed back. Content is **never** taken offline
   for mere unpopularity — only for a named standard it violates, or by
   deliberate legislative sunset.

## Article VII — The Record & Its Integrity

1. The **archive** records all governance. It is **operated by the executive**
   (Art. V) — but its **integrity is independent of its operator**, guaranteed
   not by organization but by construction: the record is **tamper-evident**
   (hash-chained, signed, externally anchored, and replicated), so that no
   operator — the executive included — can alter or destroy it *undetectably*.
2. Every integrity output is **universally verifiable** — the record, the
   verifiable random draws, the vote and confidence counts, and the canonical
   text of the law are each independently re-derivable by any member, so that
   *no party need be trusted*. Every member has the **right to verify**.
3. **No act may rest on a falsified or unverifiable record.** The judiciary
   shall void any such act, and any failure of the archive to remain
   tamper-evident, anchored, replicated, and verifiable is itself a justiciable
   violation.
4. Recording is mechanical and complete; access to sealed records is gated by
   due process, yet their integrity is provable without disclosure.
5. Tamper-evidence makes falsification **detectable, not impossible.** Against
   an operator who deletes, withholds, or forks the record in the open, the
   final guarantee is **transparency and exit** (Art. X §4) — a tampering operator
   is seen, and members may fork away.

## Article VIII — The Treasury & the Economic Firewall

1. The polity's **operating funds** (real money, for the world's real costs
   and the producers' compensation) and any **in-world economy** are separated
   by an absolute **firewall**: the two **never convert**. Real money earns no
   in-world currency, property, or advantage; in-world value redeems for no
   real money. They are two concerns with two different failure modes —
   mismanage the in-world economy and you **crash the game**; mismanage the
   operating funds and you **turn off the lights** — and the firewall holds each
   beyond the other's reach. (Art. I §§1–2.)
2. Operating surplus may fund costs, reserves, features, compensation, or be
   given away; it may **never** be distributed back to contributors in
   proportion to contribution. Stake yields no dividend and no redeemable
   claim.
3. **Subject to the firewall, the design of the in-world economy is left to
   legislation.** Its currency, its issuance, its monetary policy — or the
   choice to have none — are ordinary law; the polity may adopt, replace, or
   abolish any economic system, provided no in-world arrangement breaches the
   firewall or converts governance into financial return.
4. **In-world fiscal powers.** The legislature may **tax** in-world activity
   and **appropriate** in-world currency — a cycle (tax, budget, appropriate,
   disburse) run entirely through the in-world treasury. The **operating
   budget** of §1 is governed by the same legislature but is **real money** —
   denominated in real currency, owed to real creditors, bound by real-world law
   — so its failure is real (the lights go out, not a game-over screen). Its
   execution **may** itself be modeled and automated in the codebase — at high
   fidelity the in-world record of a creditor and the real creditor become one,
   and the fiction stops being fiction — yet no modeling dissolves the
   **firewall** (§1): the two denominations never convert. What is human in
   either cycle is the **administration** — the judgment to tax, spend, or pay —
   never the bookkeeping (Art. V §9).

## Article IX — Rights  **[OPEN]**

> **Recommendation.** The substantive bill of rights is **adopted by the
> ratifying convention** (Art. XI), not dictated by the founder — for the
> rights a people hold against their own polity are theirs to choose, not to
> receive. Below ratification an instance runs on operator discretion,
> disciplined by transparency and by the structural exit already guaranteed
> (Art. X §4); the founder enumerates no rights in the interim.
>
> The founder's **recommended starter set** for the convention — binding on no
> one until ratified, and amendable thereafter only under Art. X — is: **due
> process**; **free political expression**, including criticism of the
> government and its founder; **property** in one's creations and holdings;
> and **privacy** (records gated, integrity preserved). Two protections often
> grouped with rights need not wait, because the machine already secures them
> independently of any vote: **freedom from disenfranchisement** (Art. I §5)
> and **exit with one's data and creations** (Art. X §4). The convention's
> real work is therefore the civil-rights set above.
>
> **Rights here are bindings on the tools, not new machinery.** The executive
> has already built the apparatus (Art. V §8, Art. VI) and a community already
> runs it — a streamer hearing their own ban appeals *is* the judiciary at a
> jury pool of one. What ratification decides is how much **operator
> discretion** over that apparatus is surrendered. **Due process**, ratified,
> widens the judicial jury pool from the operator to a **sortition of equals**
> and binds the operator to the verdict (Art. VI §5) — it introduces no new
> process, it removes the override. **Free political expression**, likewise,
> compels the deliberation surface and bars sanction for protected speech. The
> convention invents nothing; it decides where the operator's discretion ends.
>
> Whatever is ratified should carry the rule that **no member may be
> sanctioned for the content of protected expression, nor judged by reputation
> rather than by the merits of the matter.**

## Article X — Amendment & Entrenchment

1. Law changes in tiers of increasing difficulty: **ordinary law** (majority
   of chambers); **organic law** (supermajority of the affected chamber);
   **constitutional amendment**; and **eternity clauses**.
2. A constitutional amendment requires a **supermajority of *every*
   chamber**, **sustained** through a mandatory deliberation period, and
   **ratified by a one-member-one-vote referendum** — for justice and the
   social contract belong to all equally, though governance is weighted.
3. **Eternity clauses** — Article I, the firewall, the co-equality of the
   chambers, the **tamper-evidence and universal verifiability of the
   record**, judicial egalitarianism, and this amendment rule itself — may
   not be amended.
4. The only change to an eternity clause is to **found a new polity** (a
   fork), to which members may freely migrate.

## Article XI — Founding & Transition  **[OPEN]**

> **Recommendation.** The founder authors this constitution and governs by
> fiat while the polity is below the population threshold for self-government.
> At that threshold a **ratifying convention** is held: the membership
> ratifies the constitution by egalitarian supermajority referendum,
> converting fiat into consented law. From ratification the constitution
> binds all — the founder included — and changes only by Article X. The
> founder's controlling share dilutes by ordinary arithmetic as members join;
> the **structure**, not the founder, is what is entrenched. The threshold
> should be population-based, not date-based.
>
> **The founding-stake bound.** A founding operator **may** hold a controlling
> stake during the fiat phase — but only on three conditions, which bind from
> the moment money is first taken: the stake is fixed by a **published
> formula**, never discretionary self-grant; it is **enforced by code and
> recorded tamper-evidently**, so every unit is independently verifiable; and
> it **auto-sunsets at ratification**, after which the founder earns only what
> **legislation grants as wage** and holds no privileged stake. The specific
> formula is not kernel text — it is the instance's published **founding
> charter** (for this polity, [founding-charter.md](./founding-charter.md)).
> Before ratification there is no law to bind the founder; what binds them is
> exactly this — code, publication, and exit — the same floor that disciplines
> every operator.

## Article XII — Emergency Powers  **[OPEN]**

> **Recommendation.** In a genuine crisis (security breach, raid, technical
> failure) the executive may take **narrow, protective, restorative** actions
> without prior authorization — never policy, never against political
> opponents. Every emergency act is **logged to the archive**, **auto-sunsets**
> after a short fixed period unless re-authorized by the legislature, and is
> **judicially reviewable after the fact**. Emergency powers may not amend the
> constitution, suspend rights beyond what protection requires, nor extend an
> officeholder's term. The root-power floor (the operator's unremovable
> capacity) is the final backstop, disciplined as ever by transparency and
> exit. Prefer auto-sunset + post-hoc review over pre-authorization — speed in
> the crisis, accountability after it.

## Article XIII — Interpretation  **[OPEN]**

> **Recommendation.** The **judiciary** interprets this constitution through
> constitutional review, reading the archived record of its making for
> intent, and may void acts and laws that infringe it. The **integrity
> branch** attests its canonical text. The legislature's only override of an
> interpretation is **amendment** (Art. X). Eternity clauses are interpreted
> most strictly; their only revision is by founding anew (Art. X §4).

## Schedule of Parameters

The articles above are the **logic**; this Schedule is the **configuration.**
Every tunable value the constitution depends on is named here once, with its
**change-tier** (how hard it is to alter) and its **value**, which is **set at
ratification** — except the game-balance rows marked *calibrate at launch*,
which are organic-law values tuned against a running game and never honestly
knowable in advance. A clause that names a parameter (e.g. *the passage
threshold*, *a bill's maximum lifespan*) refers to its row here. Tiers map to
Article X: **organic** = supermajority of the affected chamber; **amendment** =
supermajority of every chamber + referendum; **eternity** = unamendable
(Art. X §3). Rows pinned to "majority of chambers" are eternity-fixed by
co-equality (Art. I §5), shown for completeness, not tunable.

### Franchise & voting (Art. III–IV)

| Parameter | Meaning | Tier | Value |
|---|---|---|---|
| `influence.cap_curve` | reservoir ceiling as a (concave) function of lifetime contribution | organic | calibrate at launch |
| `influence.regen_rate` | how fast a reservoir refills with continued contribution | organic | calibrate at launch |
| `influence.capital_curve` | the (concave) dollar→capital-influence curve | organic | calibrate at launch |
| `vote.build_period` | time an allocation must rest to reach full conviction weight | organic | calibrate at launch |
| `vote.decay_rate` | how fast weight decays when an allocation is moved | organic | calibrate at launch |
| `vote.passage_threshold` | per-house weight a bill must hold to carry | amendment | set at ratification |
| `vote.threshold_basis` | base the threshold is measured against — votes cast vs. eligible weight | amendment | set at ratification |
| `vote.quorum` | minimum active *membership* for a house's decisions to count; below it the house abstains | amendment | set at ratification |
